EMPLOYEE RIGHTS IN INDIA - GENERAL INFORMATION
IMPORTANT: This document is general information only. It is not legal advice. Rules differ by state, company size and type of work, and laws change. For a specific situation, contact HR, the state Labour Department or a qualified lawyer.

Last reviewed: [DATE]. Reviewed against official sources listed at the end.

1. Overview of the Labour Codes
India's central labour laws have been consolidated into four Labour Codes: the Code on Wages, 2019; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; the Code on Social Security, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. They came into force on 21 November 2025 and replaced 29 older central laws. Some details depend on state rules, and the Shops and Establishments Act of the state also applies to many offices.

2. Right to Wages
Employees have the right to be paid at least the minimum wage notified for their type of work and state. Wages must be paid on time, and for monthly-paid employees this is by the 7th day of the following month. Deductions from wages are allowed only for reasons permitted by law.

3. Equal Pay and No Gender Discrimination
Employers must not discriminate on the basis of gender in wages for the same work or work of a similar nature, or in recruitment and conditions of service.

4. Overtime
Work beyond the normal working hours must be paid at not less than twice the normal rate of wages. Normal working hours under the OSH Code are generally capped at 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week. Actual limits and overtime rules may vary by state.

5. Weekly Rest, Leave and Holidays
Employees are entitled to a weekly day of rest, paid annual leave earned by days worked, and public holidays as per the state or company holiday list. The exact leave count depends on the law, the state and the company policy. Where company policy is more generous than the law, the company policy applies.

6. Appointment Letter and Written Terms
Employees have the right to a written appointment letter stating designation, wages, working hours and other terms of employment.

7. Provident Fund (EPF)
Eligible employees and their employers contribute to the Employees' Provident Fund, which supports retirement savings. Coverage depends on establishment size and wage limits set by law. Employees can check their PF balance through the official EPFO member portal.

8. Gratuity
Gratuity is a payment made by the employer in recognition of long service. Under the Code on Social Security, gratuity is generally payable after 5 years of continuous service, with special rules for some categories such as fixed-term employees. The amount is calculated by a formula based on the last drawn salary and years of service.

9. Maternity Benefit
Women employees in covered establishments are entitled to paid maternity leave, which is 26 weeks for the first two children under the law. Employers must not dismiss a woman because she is on maternity leave. Rules for later children and for crèche facilities apply as per law.

10. Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH)
Every employee has the right to a workplace free from sexual harassment. Under the POSH Act, 2013, employers with 10 or more employees must set up an Internal Committee to receive and inquire into complaints. Complaints are handled confidentially. A complaint to the Internal Committee is generally to be made within 3 months of the incident, with extensions permitted in some cases.

11. Safe and Healthy Workplace
Employers must provide a safe and healthy working environment as required by the OSH Code. Employees can report unsafe conditions to HR or the relevant authority.

12. Termination, Notice and Retrenchment
Employees can generally expect the notice period or pay in lieu of notice stated in their appointment letter. In establishments covered by the Industrial Relations Code, retrenchment (layoff for reasons other than misconduct) comes with notice and compensation requirements that depend on company size and years of service. Dismissal for misconduct requires a fair process. The exact rules depend on the type of establishment and worker category.

13. Full and Final Settlement
When an employee leaves, the employer must settle pending wages and dues within the time set by law. The settlement usually includes salary due, leave encashment where applicable, and gratuity or PF as eligible. The time limit under the new Codes should be checked against the current rules before relying on it.

14. Where to Raise a Complaint
Employees should first raise concerns with HR or the reporting manager. If unresolved, they can approach the state Labour Department or Labour Commissioner. Complaints about sexual harassment go to the Internal Committee, and PF issues can be raised with EPFO.

15. Limits of This Document
This document does not cover every situation, every state, or special categories such as contract, gig or platform workers in detail. It must be updated when the law or the company's policy changes.
